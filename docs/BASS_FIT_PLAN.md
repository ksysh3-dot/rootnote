# "저음 맞추기" (bass fit): in-app listening tests

Status: 2026-10-24. Engine knob (step 0), the pure test logic (`pvb/BassFit.kt`, `pvb/BassFitSession.kt`; host tests
`jvm/BassFitTest.kt`, `jvm/BassFitSessionTest.kt`) and the screen (`devmode/BassFitActivity.kt`, `BassFitScreen.kt`,
opened from the top of developer mode) done; not yet tried on a phone. First shipped in developer mode only; opened to
everyone after the S26 Ultra result matches the UMIK-1 measurement.

## Goal

Let the user find, by ear and blind, the settings their own phone needs: where the speaker stops (floor), how much
virtual bass, where harmonics break up, and (optionally) the harmonic shape. No third-party data needed.

## Common rules

- **Volume fixed** at the user's usual listening volume, recorded with the result; a volume change pauses the test.
  The volume link scales the result for other volumes.
- **Signals generated by the app** (no copyrighted audio). The final check uses the user's own playing music.
- **Blind:** A/B/X order random every trial; catch trials (silence, layer off) mixed in, at least two per staircase.
- **A new order every run:** each run draws a fresh seed (SecureRandom); every part takes its own seed from it, so the
  catch trials' places, the pairs' order and the A/B/X sides differ each time. The seed is shown with the result
  ("시험 코드") and kept with a saved fit, so a run can be replayed exactly.
- **ABX decision:** up to 8 trials; "distinguishable" at 7/8 or better (one-sided binomial p < 0.05); stop early at 6/6.
- **Consistency:** repeated pairs; under 70 % agreement, the step's result is not applied (defaults kept).
- **"No difference" keeps the lower / default value.** Safety caps always apply: low-mid boost <= +3 dB, virtual bass
  <= break-up limit - 3 dB, test signals below the S26 Ultra's measured protection threshold.
- Results are saved as "my fit", separate from the defaults, reversible, and included in the settings share code.

## Noise gate (strict)

Before step 1, with the phone microphone (the permission the measurement features already use):

1. Record 3 s of the room with nothing playing.
2. Play the test reference (the test band at test level) for 2 s and record it.
3. Per third-octave band of the test (63-500 Hz): the margin, and how much the room level jumps (100 ms frames:
   max - median). In the bands the speaker plays (within 20 dB of its strongest test band) the margin is the
   signal-to-noise ratio. A phone speaker barely plays 63-100 Hz, so the ratio there would fail in any room; those
   bands are judged by the room against the speaker's strongest band instead (a rumble there still masks the tones
   above it, and fails).
4. **Pass only if** every margin >= 35 dB and the room level jumps by <= 6 dB. Everything is a ratio through one
   microphone (UNPROCESSED where offered, else VOICE_RECOGNITION), so an uncalibrated phone microphone is enough. The
   same recording gives the latency (where the reference starts), used to find each trial's silent interval.
5. Otherwise: "주변이 시끄러워서 결과가 부정확할 수 있어요! 그래도 진행할까요?" [조용한 곳에서 다시] [그래도 진행].
   Proceeding marks the result "noisy" (lower confidence; never used as shared data).
6. No microphone permission: "주변이 조용한지 확인할 수 없어요" with the same choice.
7. During step 1 the silent interval of each two-interval trial is monitored; a noise burst 6 dB over the room in a
   test band discards that trial and repeats it (three times at most, then it is answered).

## Steps

| Step | What it finds | Method | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Speaker floor (lowest note the speaker itself plays cleanly) | First the listener's own threshold for a 300 Hz tone at this volume (same two-interval question, 2-down/1-up on level, 6 then 3 dB steps); then two-interval forced choice ("어느 쪽에서 소리가 났나요?"), tone bursts 10 dB over that threshold (plus the ISO 226 hearing-threshold difference to 300 Hz), from 300 Hz down in 1/6-octave steps, 2-down/1-up staircase (Levitt 1971; QUEST/Psi possible later), option "윙·지직만 났어요" counts as not heard; optional phone-mic bass check alongside. Then ABX: edge at floor vs floor x 1.3 on a 150-250 Hz bass line; no difference keeps the lower | 3 min |
| 2 | Is the layer audible; preferred strength | ABX off vs +4; then blind A/B "better / no difference" climbing over 0..+9 dB in 3 dB steps from the middle (3 vs 6, then 0 vs 3 or 6 vs 9; 2 dB steps were too close to tell apart on the S26 Ultra); ties take the lower; the first pair is asked again for consistency | 2 min |
| 3 | Break-up limit | Three answers on the bass clip: "깨끗해요 / 떨려요 / 지직거려요" (clean = the note alone, a hum felt in the hand included; rattle = a "brrr"/clatter added on the beat, something loose touching and shaking along; buzz = the sound itself cracks or tears; from tablet tests, where the two come apart). A "기준" pad plays the same clip with the layer off (what a catch trial plays) to compare with; the text says that bass louder and fuller than the reference is normal (clean), because a first-time listener reads any difference as "떨림". Either kind counts as break-up for the staircase; the result also names the kind heard at the lower levels ("먼저 나는 소리"), and break-up of either kind with the layer off warns that the volume alone is too high. Bass clip, layer 0..12 dB in 2 dB steps, 1-up/1-down from the preferred strength (converges where break-up is heard half the time); catch trials with the layer off (a "buzz" there = false alarm; over a third = unreliable). Break-up = mean of the last four reversals; limit = break-up - 3 dB, whole dB. Clean three times in a row at 12 dB and never buzzing there: no limit. (Not an ABX of a level against 6 dB lower: any audible layer differs from itself by 6 dB, so that ABX only finds the levels differ.) | 2 min |
| 4 (optional, precision) | Harmonic shape | Two knobs only: tilt (existing roll-off value) and low-order weight (H2 vs H3-5, existing harmonic offsets). 3 x 3 grid, ~18-20 blind pairs chosen where the ranking is uncertain, Bradley-Terry scores; Gaussian-process preference learning later if needed | 5-7 min |
| Check | New vs previous settings on real music | Live blind A/B (existing) | 1 min |

## What each result sets

| Result | Settings |
|---|---|
| Speaker floor F | high-pass = max(80, F / 1.5) (100 Hz at the S26 Ultra's 150); generator edge = 190 x F/150, at most 280 (1.3 times that when the edge check told them apart and the higher was preferred twice); output band low edge = 150 x F/150; "배음 표 기준 바닥" = F (developer value `table_floor`, 115-260 Hz; engine `P_D_TABLE_SCALE` = F/150). Developer mode draws F as the speaker floor in its graphs. Unreliable floor: none of these change |
| Preferred strength | virtual bass strength (capped by step 3) |
| Break-up limit | strength cap (the volume link is held off during the test and left as it is) |
| Harmonic shape | roll-off offset, harmonic offsets k = 2..5 |

## Engine (step 0, done)

`P_D_TABLE_SCALE` (index 56; 1 or 0 = model, 0.75..1.75): the virtual-bass weight/gain table is looked up at
f / scale, and the table's upper frequency clamp moves with it. The generator edge may now go up to 280 Hz. Default
and zero are bit-identical to the model (test_devmode "in tree"); a moved value changes the output.

First host measurement (layer level re the source band, dB; model = S26 Ultra):

| Note | 55 | 98 | 131 | 147 | 165 | 185 | 200 | 220 | 240 Hz |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Model | -17 | -9 | -13 | -23 | -33 | -34 | none | none | none |
| Floor 200 (scale 1.33, edge 253, out low 200) | -24 | -23 | -16 | -18 | -21 | -27 | -35 | -44 | -45 |

The scale fills 147-240 Hz as intended but also lowers 55-98 Hz notes, because the table's level matching is tied to
the S26 Ultra's speaker. Before the scale becomes a default, check it with a frequency-shifted speaker model
(`study/phone_model.py`): the perceived bass on a "floor 200" speaker with the scale should match the S26 Ultra's with
the model.

## Screen (developer mode)

Intro (volume, quiet room, nothing changes before saving; the last saved fit and "맞추기 전으로 되돌리기") -> room
check -> questions, answers in a fixed bottom bar, buttons enabled once the clips were heard (X for an ABX, both A and
B for a pair, the clip for a buzz question, the whole trial for step 1) -> result (what was found, the values that
change) -> the listener's own music, three blind pairs of the current and the new settings (optional) -> save.

- A volume change, another output or the engine stopping pauses (the sound stops) and repeats the question when back.
- Clips restart from their beginning with the side's settings (the engine takes them 200 ms before the clip).
- Saving writes the developer values (and developer mode on) and keeps the values before it, the record line and the
  floor (pvb_flags); "맞추기 전으로 되돌리기" writes them back. Leaving the screen stores nothing; leaving during the
  questions starts over, a finished run (result, music check, save) waits while another app is open.
- The screen stays on during a run. A step-1 trial disturbed three times in a row is answered anyway (a steady noise
  must not stall the test). The microphone runs only during the room check and step 1.

## Microphone reference ("마이크가 듣는 값", reference only)

The phone microphone sits centimetres from the speaker: its dB means nothing, but ratios inside one recording do.
A probe sweep (40-500 Hz log, 9 s, -15 dBFS, the speaker alone: no layer, flat EQ) is analysed per 1/6 octave
(`pvb/SpeakerProbe.kt`): the fundamental against the loudest bin (more than 10 dB down = weak) and against its 2nd/3rd
harmonics (harmonics louder = buzz only). Reference floor = the lowest bin of the unbroken "plays" run up to 400 Hz
(none if the top is not a clear tone or the recording clipped). The sweep frequency is refined to the strongest tone
within 0.2 octave, so the latency only has to be roughly known.

- Developer mode: chip "스피커 확인 (마이크)" under the test sounds, live: the frequency now, the note re the loudest
  part and re its harmonics, bars per 1/6 octave, the reference floor. Stops with any other test sound or A/B hold.
- Bass fit: the room check plays the sweep once (about 10 s more); the result shows the microphone's floor next to the
  ear's and a note when they are more than 1/3 octave apart. Never shown during the questions (it would give away
  the answer of a blind trial) and never applied.
- Known limits: the microphone's own high-pass (it hears less than is there below about 100 Hz), vibration through the
  body, clipping at high volume (flagged). Host test (SpeakerProbeTest): a simulated speaker's -10 dB point found
  within 1/6 octave at 113-197 Hz with a microphone high-pass at 80 Hz; level and a 0.3 s latency error do not move it.

## Validation against the UMIK-1

Validation mode (bass fit screen, "검증 모드"; `pvb/BassFitValidation.kt`):
- **Repeated listening runs:** step 1 or step 3 alone (`FitScope`), under virtual speakers of known floor: the
  speaker as it is, or the engine's high-pass at 120/150/180/220 Hz (2nd order) on top of the test settings. The
  chosen conditions x 1/2/3/5 repeats are shuffled (no condition twice in a row), hidden from the listener, each run
  with a fresh seed; the room is checked once per block; no result is shown between runs (a break screen only).
- **UMIK-1 sounds:** per condition, shown: 20 s pink noise (40-4000 Hz, -22 dBFS RMS) for REW's RTA with the UMIK-1
  at the ears, then the probe sweep with the phone microphone (its reference floor is logged).
- **Log:** one CSV row per run in the app's files (`bass_fit_validation.csv`; copy, send, clear): time, model, app
  version, kind (ear_floor_hz / ear_breakup_db / mic_floor_hz), condition, seed, volume, value, reliable, questions,
  catch trials, false alarms, microphone floor, clipped, latency, room pass and margin.

Analysis (outside the app):
- criterion: the UMIK-1 -10 dB point per condition (from the RTA) against the ear floor and the microphone floor
  (correlation, slope, mean difference, Bland-Altman); the UMIK-1 distortion knee against the break-up level; UMIK-1
  band levels against the room check (pass/fail agreement in quiet, fan, TV rooms);
- reliability: ICC and standard error of measurement per condition across repeats, kappa for categorical results;
- preferences (strength, shape) have no physical criterion: test-retest and the blind music check.
One phone and one listener: the result holds for this phone and this ear; more listeners show the spread between
people.

## Test logic check (simulated listeners, BassFitTest)

- Guessing listener judged "distinguished" in 3.6 % of ABX runs; a 95 %-right listener in 94 %.
- Floor staircase: median error 0.08-0.09 octave, 90th percentile 0.25-0.29 octave (true floors 130-240 Hz,
  logistic listener); a listener who "hears" silent catch trials is marked unreliable.
- Room check: a quiet room passes, a TV-like room and a voice burst fail; a speaker silent below 125 Hz in a quiet
  room passes; a 63 Hz rumble 28 dB under the strongest band fails. Latency found within 10 ms.
- Strength: lands on the level nearest the listener's ideal; ties keep the lower; a reversed repeat is flagged.
- Break-up (yes/no): median error 0.5 dB, 90th percentile 1-1.5 dB (break-up at 5-11.5 dB); limits at least 1 dB
  under the break-up in 193-200 of 200 runs; "buzz" on catch trials -> unreliable in 100 of 100.
- Session: whole runs with simulated listeners land on the floor, preferred strength, cap and shape they were given;
  the same seed replays the same questions, another seed changes the order and the sides.
- Shape grid: the preferred cell found in 37-40 of 40 runs with 18 pairs (noisy listener).

## Validation finding 2026-10-24: step 1 measured audibility, not the speaker floor

One validation run (hidden 220 Hz high-pass on a Galaxy S26 Ultra, ordinary room) found the floor at 126 Hz: full-level
bursts stay audible about 30 dB down. Step 1 now plays its bursts 10 dB over the listener's own 300 Hz threshold
(RefLevelStaircase + FloorStaircase.levelDb), so a note stops being heard where the speaker plays 10 dB less than at
300 Hz: the -10 dB point the published measurements use. Simulated listener (tools/pvb-harness/jvm/BassFitTest.kt):
hidden high-pass 120/150/180/220 Hz found within a median 0.05-0.07 octave of the -10 dB point (90th 0.11-0.15) when
unheard trials are answered "neither"; with forced guesses 0.1-0.14 (90th about 0.5). The old way found 112 Hz for
220 Hz in the same simulation. The room gate is relaxed to 15 dB (an ordinary closed room measured 18): moderate
noise raises the reference and the bursts alike.

Later finding (0.9.0-beta.10): "raises the reference and the bursts alike" holds only for noise with the shape of the
hearing threshold. A room's noise is mostly in the mids, so it raises the 300 Hz threshold more than a low note's; the
bursts then play too loud and the floor comes out low (a run in an ordinary room at 30 % volume found 112 Hz, the
microphone 160 Hz). Low bursts can also be felt in the hand before they are heard; step 1 now asks for what is heard
and counts a felt hum as "only a feel or noise". An optional "소음 보정" (NoiseMasking, off by default) adds back the
difference: each threshold's rise is the room's third-octave level (dB SPL by Android's nominal input sensitivity,
90 dB SPL = RMS 2500 of 32768) as a masked tone 2 dB under the band, power-added to the ISO 226 threshold; the burst
moves by its rise minus the 300 Hz one, limited to -12..+6 dB. Uncalibrated, so the validation log keeps the value at
100 Hz (`noise_corr_100_db`) to check against the UMIK-1.

Correction (0.9.0-beta.11): the room check records UNPROCESSED where the phone offers it, and the CDD gives that source
its own sensitivity (94 dB SPL = -36 dB re full scale, flat within 10 dB from 100 Hz to 7 kHz, 20 dB below), 17.6 dB
apart from VOICE_RECOGNITION's. beta.10 used the VOICE_RECOGNITION value for both, so with UNPROCESSED the room read
17.6 dB too quiet and the correction hardly acted. NoiseMasking now takes the offset of the source actually opened,
ignores the bands under 100 Hz, and the log records the source (`mic_source`). Still nominal values: one recording of
the room beside the UMIK-1 gives the real offset of a phone.

Microphone response (next version): what bass fit measures with the phone microphone (the room bands of the noise
correction, the probe sweep's fundamental and harmonics) now has the typical microphone response taken off
(MicResponse): the median of published measurements of 53 Android phones, and of the 11 Galaxy phones among them for
Samsung, re 1 kHz, 100 Hz-2 kHz, smoothed and rounded to 0.5 dB (all within 3 dB). Across phones the response varies
by about 6 dB (10-90 %) at 160-315 Hz, 10-12 dB at 100-125 Hz and about 20 dB at 63 Hz and below, so the median takes
off the typical tilt only; nothing below 100 Hz is corrected. The noise gate and the disturbance check compare levels
of one microphone in one band and stay uncorrected.
